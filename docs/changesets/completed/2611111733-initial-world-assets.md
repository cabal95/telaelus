# Initial World Assets

- Status: Implemented
- Id: 2611111733-initial-world-assets
- Depends-on: none

## Summary

Replaces the last placeholder visuals with generated low-poly models: a ground tile per terrain
(three variants each), transition edge and corner pieces, a river piece per river shape and bank
terrain, and a model per building type. It also starts an **art style blueprint** that records
how every asset in the game should look and be built, so later assets (roads, pipes, bridges,
vehicles, new buildings) follow one set of rules.

## Motivation

The ground, rivers, and buildings are still colored boxes. Mountains and ore already come from
the asset factory, and the trees and character are CC0 imports, so the world mixes real models
with placeholders and has no written style for new assets to match. The game plan lists "art
style and how we source art" as an open question; this changeset answers it for the on-planet
game.

## Design

### Art style blueprint (new: `docs/blueprints/art-style.md`)

A new blueprint area, the authority on how assets look and are made. Its content:

- **Look:** low-poly, flat-shaded (faceted, no smoothing), flat colors from material diffuse
  values, no textures. Emission only for glowing materials (lava, furnace mouths), at half the
  base color. The character is the one textured asset, an accepted CC0 exception. Small surface
  details stay nearly flat: the game's dark ambient turns any steep face away from the sun black,
  so small steep details read as black specks.
- **View:** an orthographic camera at yaw 45 and pitch -48 with one shadowed directional light;
  the tilt comes from the camera, not the art. Assets are judged at the default zoom from that
  angle (silhouette and color first, detail second).
- **Palette:** the base color of each terrain, water, lava, stone, snow, wood, metal, and the
  building accent colors, as one table. Generators use these values; new materials pick from the
  table or extend it.
- **Scale:** 1 fine cell = 1 unit, 1 map cell = 4 x 4 units, and the size targets for the player
  (about 1.4 tall), ore (0.8), trees (2.5 to 3.5), mountains (3.2 to 3.5), and buildings (below).
- **Ground levels:** land surfaces sit at y = 0 with relief under 0.04 and none within about half
  a unit of an edge; water and lava surfaces sit at y = -0.1. Land tiles carry a short skirt from 0
  down to -0.1 on every edge, hidden between two land tiles and showing as a bank beside water or
  lava, so no liquid tile needs per-neighbor geometry. Transition pieces sit 0.006 above the
  ground they cover, with walls down to -0.1 (including on their outer edge, in their own color,
  so the gap under them never shows the tile beneath as a hairline). Flat ground pieces cast no
  shadows.
- **Cell conventions:** a cell-filling model is 4 x 4 units, origin at the center of its base, and
  stays inside its cell. Its edges either lie flat (mountains, ground tiles) or follow a shared
  profile documented for its family (the river channel), so any variant fits beside any other in
  any 90-degree rotation.
- **Variants:** three variants (a, b, c) where repetition shows (ground, mountains, ore, trees),
  chosen with a 90-degree yaw from the map cell coordinates; one model where a thing has a single
  identity (each building, each river shape).
- **Sources:** generated by the asset factory first (`docs/asset-pipeline.md`). CC0 imports are
  allowed when they match the look; they keep their source noted in their folder README.
- **Naming and requirement:** `snake_case` file names by family (`ground_grass_a.glb`,
  `river_bend_sand.glb`, `smelter.glb`). Models are required: a missing model throws. The
  character, already the textured exception, is the only exception here too.

### Ground tiles

- One model per terrain and variant: `ground_<terrain>_<a|b|c>.glb` for grass, sand, snow, rock,
  mud, shallow_water, deep_water, and lava (24 files) in `src/Game/assets/models/terrain/`.
- Land tiles (grass, sand, snow, rock, mud) are a gently faceted 4 x 4 surface at y = 0 with small,
  nearly flat terrain details that never matter to gameplay: grass patches, sand ripples, snow
  drifts, rock slabs and pebbles, mud puddles. Every edge vertex is at y = 0, plus the skirt to
  -0.1.
- Liquid tiles (shallow and deep water, lava) are a faceted surface at y = -0.1; deep water is
  darker than shallow, and lava has glowing cracks between darker crust plates (emission).
- A mountain cell and a river cell do not draw a ground tile: the mountain model and the river
  piece each cover their whole cell.

### Transitions

- The draw-priority rule is unchanged: on each edge whose neighbor outranks the cell, the cell
  draws a strip of the neighbor's terrain, and each corner draws a square of the highest-ranked
  terrain around it. The pieces become models:
  - `edge_<terrain>.glb`: a strip along one edge, between the corners, with a ragged inner edge.
  - `edge_mouth_<terrain>.glb`: the same strip with the 2-unit gap in the middle where a river
    flows in.
  - `corner_<terrain>.glb`: the corner square, ragged on its two inner sides.
- Only terrains that can outrank a neighbor need pieces: grass, snow, sand, mud, rock, and lava
  (18 files). A piece sits at its own terrain's surface level, so a grass strip in a water cell
  is a ragged shore lip at y = 0 with a skirt down to the water.
- Each piece is authored for the north edge (or the north-west corner) and rotated into place.

### Rivers

- One model per river shape and bank terrain: `river_<shape>_<terrain>.glb` for the shapes pool,
  end, straight, bend, junction, and cross, and the bank terrains grass, sand, snow, mud, and
  rock (30 files).
- A piece covers its whole cell: banks in the terrain's look at y = 0 (with the land skirt), and a
  channel matching the sim's (the middle 2 x 2 fine cells plus a 2-wide arm to each connected
  edge). The water surface is at y = -0.1 with short sloped sides, so it meets a neighboring
  piece or a water tile flush.
- Every connected edge has the same channel cross-section, so pieces join across cells.
- Canonical orientation: end connects north; straight runs north-south; bend connects north and
  east; junction connects north, east, and south. The renderer rotates the piece to the cell's
  connections. Straight is the piece a future bridge sits on.

### Buildings

One model per type in `src/Game/assets/models/buildings/`, authored facing North (front toward -Z),
filling its footprint minus the 0.1 inset per side, rotated with the building's facing:

| Type | File | Footprint (N) | Height | Look |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mine | `mine.glb` | 2 x 2 | about 1.4 | a timber headframe over a dark shaft opening, ore cart at the front, orange accents |
| Lumber Camp | `lumber_camp.glb` | 2 x 2 | about 1.2 | a plank shed with a log pile and saw bench at the front, brown accents |
| Smelter | `smelter.glb` | 3 x 3 | about 2.2 | a stone furnace block with a chimney and a glowing mouth at the front, red accents |
| Refinery | `refinery.glb` | 2 x 3 | about 2.0 | two tanks joined by pipes with a small control hut at the front, blue accents |

- The front marks the building's facing (where belt output will go later), so a rotated building
  reads as rotated.
- The accent colors keep today's identity colors (orange, brown, red, blue), so buildings remain
  recognizable at a glance.
- **The build ghost is a translucent copy of the real model**, rotated with the chosen facing,
  so the player sees the building and its front before placing it. Every surface of the copy
  takes one tint material, green when placeable and red when not, at about 45% opacity, so the
  verdict reads the same on any building. A thin translucent plate in the same tint covers the
  exact footprint at ground level, since the model's silhouette alone does not show cell edges.

### Code shape

- A shared `ModelLibrary` holds the required-model rules now in `MountainModels`: a `PackedScene`
  cache, a throw naming the path when a file is missing or not a `Node3D`, and the map-cell hash
  that picks a variant and a 90-degree yaw.
- `TerrainTiles` builds each cell from models: the ground tile (unless a mountain or river covers
  the cell), the edge and corner pieces from the same priority rules, the river piece rotated to
  its connections, and the mountain via `MountainModels`. Ground tiles, edges, corners, and river
  pieces have shadow casting switched off (`ModelLibrary.WithoutShadows`); mountains keep it.
- `BuildingViews` instances the type's model at the footprint center rotated by facing, instead of
  a box.

## Code changes

### Asset factory

- `src/asset-factory/tools/mesh.py` (new): the shared building blocks moved out of `gen.py`
  (vector helpers and the `Mesh` writer), plus `PALETTE` (the values the blueprint lists), a
  `Materials` collector for palette colors and shades, and simple solids (box, cylinder, spike,
  gabled roof).
- `src/asset-factory/tools/terrain_gen.py` (new): ground tiles, transition pieces, and river
  pieces. `src/asset-factory/tools/building_gen.py` (new): the four buildings.
  `src/asset-factory/tools/gen.py` keeps mountains and ore and runs every generator.
- `src/asset-factory/tools/arrange.py` (new): composes small test maps from the terrain models the
  way `TerrainTiles` places them, as one OBJ for `preview.py`.
- `src/asset-factory/models/`: the new sources. `src/asset-factory/previews/`: two arrangement
  sheets (`arrangement_ground`, which also shows the transitions, and `arrangement_rivers`) and one
  sheet per building.

### Game assets

- `src/Game/assets/models/terrain/`: 72 new `.glb` files with their `.import` files (24 ground, 18
  transition, 30 river); the README lists every family, its naming, and its edge rules.
- `src/Game/assets/models/nature/README.md`: the models are required; the fallback text is
  removed.
- `src/Game/assets/models/buildings/`: four `.glb` files with `.import` files and a README.

### Presentation

- New `src/Game/Presentation/ModelLibrary.cs` (required loading, cache, cell hash, variant and yaw
  per cell, edge yaw, `WithoutShadows`).
- `MountainModels.cs`: uses `ModelLibrary`.
- `TerrainTiles.cs`: rebuilt on models as above; the colored boxes and the color table are
  removed.
- New `src/Game/Presentation/RiverPieces.cs`: maps a cell's connections to a shape file and a yaw.
- `NodeModels.cs`: loads ore and tree models through `ModelLibrary`; the colored-box fallback and
  its fallback colors and widths are removed.
- `BuildingViews.cs`: instances building models; `BoxHeight` and the color table are removed (the
  ghost uses the same models, below).
- New `src/Game/Presentation/BuildingModels.cs`: the type-to-file table and `Create(type, facing)`,
  used by both `BuildingViews` and the ghost.
- `BuildController.cs`: the ghost box becomes a `BuildingModels` instance for the selected type,
  rebuilt when the selection changes and rotated with the facing, with the tint material applied
  to every mesh surface and swapped between the green and red tints; plus the footprint plate.
  `CurrentGhost()` and the snapshot report the same values as today.

### Tests

- `terrain-types.json`: add a cross with three ends on snow, a bend and a junction on mud that
  drain into shallow water (a river mouth), and a pool on rock, with a `river-shapes` screenshot;

- A new scenario `building-models.json` places all four buildings, the Refinery in all four
