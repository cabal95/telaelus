# Research: The Research Lab, Material Transfer, and the Research Tree

- Status: Implemented
- Id: 2611151236-research
- Depends-on: 2611151108-power-consumers

## Summary

A global research system with two projects to start: **Material Transfer**, which lets the HQ
network carry the basic raw resources (Wood, Stone, Iron Ore, Copper Ore, Coal) from online
collectors straight into storage, and **Advanced Material Transfer**, unlocked by it, which carries
every other raw resource. Research is not tied to a building: one project runs at a time for the
whole base, and every **Research Lab** speeds it up, each one less than the one before. Progress is
counted in game-world time (Material Transfer takes two game days with one lab) and is never lost:
a paused project keeps its progress and resumes where it stopped. Some projects charge a one-time
fee to begin. A **research tree** panel lists the projects with the link between them.

## Motivation

The resource tree (2611141757-resource-tree, sections 6, 7, and its step 6) planned research as
the step after trucks, starting with Material Transfer: the progression reads as hand collection,
then trucks, then network transfer. It was skipped until a research tree was worth designing; this
changeset builds the system with two linked projects, so the tree, prerequisites, and locked
projects all work from the start, and later projects (tier unlocks, upgrades, Part B's needs) are
new entries rather than new machinery. Splitting the transfer also keeps trucks useful into the
mid-game: the newer deposits still need hauling until the second project is done.

## Design

### Scope

- **In:** the research system in the sim (projects, prerequisites, the active project, progress,
  fees, research speed from labs); the Research Lab and its model; Material Transfer and Advanced
  Material Transfer and their effects; the research tree panel with its link; the lab's panel rows;
  the projects' icons.
- **Out:** more projects (each its own changeset); tier unlocks by research;
  saving to disk (there is no save system yet; research state lives in the sim with everything
  else, ready for one); a top bar research readout (the bar is full at 1280 wide).

### Projects

- A project (`ResearchProject`: `MaterialTransfer`, `AdvancedMaterialTransfer`) has a definition
  (`ResearchDefs`): a name, a description of what it does, its **work** in game seconds at one
  lab's speed, its **fee** (resources paid once to begin; may be empty), its prerequisites (other
  projects), and its place in the tree (a column and row for the panel).

| Project | Work | Fee | Prerequisites | Transfers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Material Transfer | 1200 s (two game days at one lab) | 40 Iron, 20 Copper | none | Wood, Stone, Iron Ore, Copper Ore, Coal |
| Advanced Material Transfer | 1800 s (three game days at one lab) | 60 Steel, 20 Glass | Material Transfer | every other raw resource a collector makes: Crude Oil, Bauxite, Limestone, Quartz, Sulfur, Titanium Ore, Rare Earths, Uranium Ore, Crystals |

(placeholders; `DayLength` is 600 s.)

### Running research

- **One project at a time, for the whole base.** `Research.Active` is the running project or none.
  Each project keeps its own progress (`Research.Progress`, game seconds of work done), whether
  its fee is paid, and whether it is complete.
- **Starting** a project (`StartResearch`): it must be incomplete and its prerequisites complete.
  If its fee is unpaid, storage must hold the whole fee, which is taken then and marked paid. It
  becomes the active project; any project that was active is paused with its progress kept.
- **Pausing** (`PauseResearch`) clears the active project; progress and the paid fee stay.
  Resuming is starting again: no second fee, progress continues. The fee is never refunded, and
  never charged twice.
- **Progress:** each tick the active project gains `dt` times the **research speed**. With no lab
  working the project waits.
- **Research speed, diminishing with a floor:** the working labs (switched on and online, each at
  its work speed, so a lab in a power brownout at 80% counts 0.8 and one in a blackout or offline
  nothing) are taken fastest first; the first adds its full speed, and each one after adds half as
  much as the one before, but never less than a quarter (`LabFloor` 0.25): 1, then 0.5, then 0.25
  for every lab after. One lab is 1x (Material Transfer in two days), two 1.5x, three 1.75x, five
  2.25x. More labs always help, but research cannot be rushed far by building many.
- **Completion:** at full work the project is complete, its effect applies from then on, and no
  project is active until the player starts another.

### Material Transfer

- Once a transfer project is complete, **every online, switched-on collector** (extractors, the
  Quarry, the Deep Well and Deep Mine) drains the resources it transfers from its output buffer
  into storage each tick, as far as room allows, at once (as online processors already do).
  Material Transfer covers Wood, Stone, Iron Ore, Copper Ore, and Coal; Advanced Material Transfer
  the rest. A buffer's other resources stay for trucks and hand collection, as do offline
  collectors' buffers.
- Trucks need no change: they go to the fullest buffer at 10 or more, and a transferred buffer now
  stays near empty, so after Material Transfer they haul the newer deposits and offline outposts,
  and after Advanced Material Transfer only outposts and motherlodes beyond the network.
- Processors (the Brine Well's Lithium among them) already deliver into storage online; the
  transfer projects change only collectors.

### UI: the research tree

- **T** opens the research tree (a modal panel like the build menu; T, Escape, or its close
  button closes it). Its header shows the research speed ("Speed 1.5x: 2 labs working", or "No
  labs working: research waits" in the warning color).
- **The tree:** projects as cards on a grid by their column and row, left to right, with a line
  from each prerequisite to the project it opens (Material Transfer to Advanced Material
  Transfer), drawn in the accent color once the prerequisite is complete and dim before. A card shows the project's icon, its name, a short description, its
  fee (each resource's icon and amount; "Paid" once paid; amounts short in the short color), its
  work as game days ("2 days at 1 lab") and the time left at the current speed, a progress bar, and
  its state: Available, Running, Paused, Locked (its card dimmed, naming the prerequisite it
  waits for), or Complete. A transfer project's card also lists what it transfers, as resource
  icons.
- **Buttons on a card:** Start (pays the fee; disabled with the fee short, the button's tooltip
  naming what is missing), Pause while running, Resume while paused, nothing when complete.
- Starting a project while another runs pauses that one; the card says so in its tooltip ("Pauses
  Material Transfer").

## Verification

- Unit tests: starting a project takes its fee once and refuses with the fee short; Advanced
  Material Transfer refused until Material Transfer is complete; pausing keeps progress and the
  paid fee; resuming charges nothing and continues; starting another project pauses the first;
  research speed at one, two, three, and five labs (1, 1.5, 1.75, 2.25), in a brownout (scaled),
  and with no lab working (none); Material Transfer done at 1200 s with one lab; completion clears
  the active project; before Material Transfer an online Mine's Iron Ore fills its buffer, after it
  the buffer drains into storage and stops when storage is full, while an offline Mine still fills
  and an online Drill Rig's Bauxite still fills until Advanced Material Transfer.
- Scenario: `research` builds a Research Lab, opens the tree with T (Advanced Material Transfer
  locked), starts Material Transfer (the fee leaves storage), pauses and resumes it (no second fee,
  progress kept), runs it to completion with progress seeded near the end, sees a Mine's buffer
  drain into storage, and sees Advanced Material Transfer become available.
- Both test suites pass; a windowed run shows the lab and the tree.

## Decisions

1. **Research speed diminishes with a floor:** each working lab after the first adds half as much
   as the one before, never less than a quarter.
2. **Costs:** the lab is 40 Stone, 30 Wood, 30 Iron (no Glass); Material Transfer's fee is 40 Iron
   and 20 Copper (no Glass).
3. **Transfer is instant** for now: as fast as storage has room.
4. **T** opens the research tree.
5. **Two transfer projects:** Material Transfer carries Wood, Stone, Iron Ore, Copper Ore, and Coal
   (the ores, since collectors make ore, not metal); Advanced Material Transfer, which needs it,
   carries every other raw resource. It gives the tree a working prerequisite and keeps trucks
   useful into the mid-game.
6. **Advanced Material Transfer:** 1800 s of work (three days at one lab) and a fee of 60 Steel and
   20 Glass.
7. **The floor** is a quarter of a lab (`LabFloor` 0.25).

## Implementation notes

- **A new research.md blueprint** holds the system, the effects, and the tree; buildings.md has
  the lab and points there.
- **No `Debug.CompleteResearch`:** `Debug.SetResearchProgress` at a project's full work completes it
  (and counts its fee as paid), which covers both.
- **Progress is kept in double** inside the sim: a project runs for tens of thousands of ticks, and
  a float sum drifted by seconds over two game days. `ResearchProgress` still reads a float.
- **Switching between two running projects** is in the code (starting one pauses the other) but
  not tested yet: with the prerequisite chain only one project can be started at a time. The first
  project with a second open branch should add that test.
- **Cards are 280 x 290** with 64 units between columns; project icons are named from the project
  (`icon_material_transfer`), so a new project needs only its icon model.
- **Stockyards still matter:** Bauxite, Limestone, Quartz, and the other Bulk resources with no room
  at the HQ need a Stockyard or Silo before Advanced Material Transfer can drain them.
- **The tree's layout (reviewed):** the modal covers the window below the top bar with a margin of
  world round it; cards show only the icon, the name, and a running indicator; the details, the
  cost, and the Start Research button sit in a drawer at the right edge, showing the selected
  card. `Dialog.Place` became virtual so the tree can size itself.
