# HQ Network

- Status: Implemented
- Id: 2611142047-hq-network
- Depends-on: none

## Summary

Adds the HQ network: the HQ and two new hub buildings (the Small Hub and the Relay) link into one
network, and every building they cover is **online**; the rest are **offline**. Producing
buildings get a small local buffer. Collectors always fill their buffer, online processors use
storage directly, offline ones work from their buffers, and the player empties or loads any
building by hand at 10x hand-harvest speed. Storage buildings count only while online. A network
overlay (N) and placement coverage show what is connected.

## Motivation

This is the second follow-on of 2611141757-resource-tree (sections 6 and 7, decisions 5, 6, 7,
and 12 there). Storage now has limits, but every building still reaches it from anywhere on the
map. The network makes distance and layout matter, gives the HQ its role as the root everything
connects back to, and lays the ground the power, Water and Sand capacity, Depot truck, and
Material Transfer changesets all build on.

## Design

### Scope

- **In:** online and offline; the HQ as the root hub; the Small Hub and Relay buildings and their
  models; local buffers; collecting and loading by hand; storage buildings counting only online;
  the network overlay, coverage while placing, and online state in the UI.
- **Out:** power, Water, and Sand on the network (their changesets); Depot trucks; Material
  Transfer (research). Until trucks land, raw resources reach storage only by hand: a deliberate
  manual chore for now, including the hauling a launch needs.

### Hubs and the network

A **hub** has two radii, both in world units measured from the hub's footprint center. One world
unit is one fine cell (a map cell is 4 units across). For scale: an extractor's work radius is 6
units and the player sees 24.

- **Coverage radius:** how far the hub reaches to connect buildings. A building is covered when the
  nearest point of its footprint is within this distance of the hub's center. This is the same
  measure an extractor uses for its work radius (center to the nearest point of a field's square),
  so a large building joins as soon as any part of it is in reach.
- **Link radius:** how far the hub reaches to connect to other hubs, center to center.

| Hub | Footprint | Cost | Coverage radius | Link radius | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HQ | 6 x 10 | placed by the world | 14 (a circle 28 units across) | 16 | |
| Small Hub | 1 x 1 | 10 Wood, 5 Stone | 8 (16 across, about 4 x 4 map cells) | 12 | 0 |
| Relay | 2 x 2 | 20 Iron, 10 Stone | 3 (just its own spot) | 30 | 0 |

(Placeholders for tuning.) In practice: a Small Hub connects the buildings in a patch about 16
units across around it, a chain of Small Hubs can be spaced up to 12 units apart, and a Relay
carries the network about 30 units in one jump to reach an outpost, where a Small Hub then covers
the buildings.

- Two hubs **link** when the distance between their centers is at most the larger of their link
  radii, so a Relay reaches a Small Hub that could not reach it back.
- The network is the set of hubs linked to the HQ, directly or through other hubs. A hub in it is
  online; a hub cluster that does not link back to the HQ is not a second network, it is offline.
- A building is **online** when it is covered by an online hub (above), or it is itself an online
  hub. Everything else is offline.
- **Switched off** hubs neither link nor cover, so switching one off is how the player cuts a
  branch.
- The network is recomputed only when a building is placed, destroyed, or switched on or off: a
  flood fill over the hubs from the HQ, then a coverage pass over the buildings. Each building
  stores whether it is online. Nothing is computed per tick.

### Local buffers

Every building that makes or gathers stored resources has a local **buffer** (placeholders):

| Building | Buffer |
|---|---|
| Extractors (Mine, Lumber Camp, Oil Well) | 20 units of output, any mix of types it works |
| Processors (Smelter, Refinery) | an output buffer of 10 units, and an input buffer of 5 cycles' worth |

- **Collectors** (extractors) always put each unit into their buffer, online or not; raw resources
  reach storage by hand (and later by truck, or through Material Transfer). A full buffer stops
  the collector: Idle, "Full: waiting for pickup".
- **Online processors** take inputs from their input buffer first (so units loaded while it was
  offline are not stranded), then from storage, and put outputs into storage directly, as today.
  If storage is full the output goes to the output buffer instead, which drains back into storage
  as room appears; a full output buffer stops it with "Storage full: Iron".
- **Offline processors** take inputs only from their input buffer and put outputs into their output
  buffer. Empty inputs: "Waiting for Iron Ore (load by hand)"; a full output buffer: "Full:
  collect by hand".
- A building's buffer is kept when it goes offline or online. Destroying a building returns its
  buffers to storage in full (past limits, like every return).
- The HQ, hubs, and storage buildings have no buffer.

### Collecting and loading by hand

- The E key now works on buildings too. The **interaction target** is the nearest hand-harvestable
  field or building with something to collect or load, within 1.5 units of the player (edge
  distance, as for fields today); a building wins a tie.
- Holding E beside a building **collects**: its output buffer's units move into storage one at a
  time, each taking the resource's gather time over 10, or 0.3 s for a resource with no gather
  time. It stops when the buffer is empty or storage has no room.
- Holding E beside an **offline processor** with an empty output buffer **loads**: its recipe's
  inputs move from storage into its input buffer the same way, up to 5 cycles' worth.
- The hint shows the action: "[E] Collect Wood (12)" or "[E] Load Iron Ore (3 / 5)", with the
  progress bar, and "Storage full" when collecting has no room.
- Collecting is hand work, so it works on online and offline buildings alike; trucks will take over
  most of it later.

### Storage, online and off

- A storage building adds to limits only while online. The HQ is always online.
- A Silo, Stockyard, Tank Farm, Warehouse, or Vault placed out of reach does nothing until a hub
  covers it, and its panel says so ("Offline: adds nothing until connected").
- Stock above a limit that drops when storage goes offline is kept, as for destroying storage.

### UI

- **Network overlay (N):** toggles a ground overlay: each online hub's coverage as a translucent
  disc in the accent color, links between online hubs as lines, and offline or switched-off hubs'
  coverage as a gray disc. Offline buildings are marked by their world marker (below), which shows
  with or without the overlay, so the overlay draws no outline of its own. It is off by default.
- **Placing any building** shows the whole overlay (every hub, not only nearby ones: simpler, and
  the discs are cheap); placing a hub adds the new hub's coverage in green and lines to the hubs it
  would link to. The placement tooltip adds "Online" or "Offline (no hub in reach)".
- **A selected hub:** while the detail panel shows the HQ, a Small Hub, or a Relay, the map shows
  that hub's coverage as a translucent disc and lines to the hubs it links to, the same look as the
  overlay, whether or not the overlay is on.
- **Building detail panel:** a "Network" row, "Online" or "Offline"; a "Buffer" row ("Iron Ore 12
  / 20", or "Iron 4 / 10; in: Iron Ore 3 / 5" for a processor); for a hub, "Links" (how many hubs it
  links to) and "Reach" ("covers 8, links 12"), and no State or progress, since a hub does no work
  (it keeps Turn off, which cuts its branch).
- **The world:** an offline building that is not switched off shows a small `Warning` ring
  floating above it, so outposts read as unconnected without the overlay.
- **Hint:** the collect and load hints above.

### Assets

- **Small Hub** (1 x 1): a short mast on a square base with a glowing ring near the top in a new
  `hub_accent` (bright cyan-green), so hubs read as part of one system with the HQ's comms tower.
- **Relay** (2 x 2): a taller lattice tower with a dish and the same `hub_accent` ring.
- **Offline marker:** drawn by the presentation (a flat ring mesh), not an asset file.
- Both models with their kept preview sheets, the README rows, and the palette entry, per the
  asset pipeline.

  building joining when only a corner is in reach), switched

  toggle; extractors filling and stopping on a full buffer; online and offline processors;

- Scenarios: a `network` scenario places a Mine out of reach (offline marker, "Full: waiting for
  pickup"), collects from it by hand, places a Small Hub to bring it online, toggles the overlay,
  and checks the placement tooltip's online line; existing scenarios pass with hubs added where
  needed.
- Both test suites pass; a windowed run shows the overlay, the hub models, and the hints.

## Decisions

1. **Raw resources before trucks:** collectors never deliver to storage on their own; until the
   Depot trucks changeset, collecting is a manual chore. The build order is unchanged.
2. **Coverage measure:** the same as an extractor's work radius, from the hub's center to the
   nearest point of the building's footprint.
3. **Hub radii and costs:** the placeholders above.
4. **One E key** for harvesting fields and collecting from or loading buildings.
