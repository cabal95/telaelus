# Terrain models

Models drawn by the terrain renderer (`TerrainTiles`, `RiverPieces`, and `MountainModels` in
`src/Game/Presentation/`). See `docs/blueprints/terrain.md` for how they are placed and
`docs/blueprints/art-style.md` for the look. All are generated by the asset factory
(`src/asset-factory/tools/terrain_gen.py` and `gen.py`, see `docs/asset-pipeline.md`).

Every file here is required: the game loads them by name and throws if one is missing.

## Shared conventions

- One map cell is 4 x 4 world units; a cell model's origin is the center of its base, north is
  -Z, and nothing extends past the cell.
- Land surfaces are at y = 0, water and lava surfaces at y = -0.1. Land carries a skirt down to
  -0.1 on every open side, which shows as a bank beside a liquid.
- Ground pieces cast no shadows (the game switches it off); mountains do.

## Ground tiles (24)

`ground_<terrain>_<a|b|c>.glb` for `grass`, `sand`, `snow`, `rock`, `mud`, `shallow_water`,
`deep_water`, and `lava`.

- Land tiles are faceted surfaces with small, nearly flat details (grass patches, sand ripples,
  snow drifts, rock slabs and pebbles, mud puddles). Every edge vertex is at y = 0, and the
  surface stays flat within about half a unit of each edge, where transition strips lie.
- Liquid tiles are faceted surfaces at y = -0.1; lava has glowing cracks between darker crust
  plates.
- The variant and a 90-degree yaw are chosen per map cell from its coordinates.

## Transition pieces (18)

For each terrain that can outrank a neighbor (`grass`, `snow`, `sand`, `mud`, `rock`, `lava`):

- `edge_<terrain>.glb`: a strip along the North edge, between the corners (x from -1.6 to 1.6),
  0.4 deep at its ends with a ragged inner edge.
- `edge_mouth_<terrain>.glb`: the same strip with a gap for |x| < 1, where a river flows in.
- `corner_<terrain>.glb`: the North-West corner square (0.4 x 0.4) with a small rounded bulge.

Pieces sit 0.006 above the ground they cover (lava pieces just above -0.1), with walls down to
-0.1. The game turns them to the edge or corner they belong to.

## River pieces (30)

`river_<shape>_<bank>.glb` for the shapes `pool`, `end`, `straight`, `bend`, `junction`, and
`cross`, and the bank terrains `grass`, `sand`, `snow`, `mud`, and `rock`.

- A piece covers its whole cell: banks in the terrain's look at y = 0, and the sim's channel (the
  middle 2 x 2 fine cells plus a 2-wide arm to each connected edge) sunk to a water surface at
  y = -0.1 with sloped sides a quarter unit wide.
- Every connected edge has the same cross-section: banks at |offset| > 1, slopes down to water
  for |offset| < 0.75.
- Canonical connections: end North; straight North and South; bend North and East; junction
  North, East, and South. The game turns the piece to the cell's connections.

## Mountains (6)

| Ground beneath the mountain | Family | Files |
|---|---|---|
| Snow | Snow | `mountain_snow_a.glb`, `mountain_snow_b.glb`, `mountain_snow_c.glb` |
| Grass, Sand, Mud, Rock | Stone | `mountain_a.glb`, `mountain_b.glb`, `mountain_c.glb` |

- Both families use the same three shapes (a, b, c); only the coloring differs. Stone is bare
  rock; Snow is snow-covered with rock on the steep faces.
- Every edge of every variant lies flat on the ground (height 0), so each mountain is a
  standalone peak: it looks finished alone, and neighbors sit side by side as a cluster. Any
  variant can sit next to any other in any 90-degree rotation.
- The variant and a 90-degree rotation are chosen per map cell from its coordinates.
- Adding a family (for example sandstone for Sand) means three files plus a case in
  `MountainModels`.
